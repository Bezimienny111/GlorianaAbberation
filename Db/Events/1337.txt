While he was successful in doing so, thoroughly ridding both Sjaelland and Jutland of the Holsteiners by 1347, this endeavor placed him deeply in debt. Some of this debt was financed through extra taxation on the newly conquered peasants. Nevertheless, excessive taxation caused greater rebellion hindering his ability to consolidate power. In order to relieve some of this economic pressure on himself and his countrymen, Valdemar made a deal in 1346 to sell Denmark's piece of Estonia to the Teutonic Order, allowing him to pay off debts in parts of Denmark that were culturally Danish. Had Valdemar elected to keep control of Estonia, his financial troubles likely would have deepened, as he struggled to reclaim complete control in this relatively distant province."
